  浮梁民程發為人傭，乃屢往來江浙間。淳熙十四年九月自臨安歸，過黔縣境，清旦遇婦人於塗，顔狀愁悴，告程曰：「我不幸丈夫狠惡，常遭鞭箠，而阿婆性尤嚴暴，不曾得一日定歇。昨夜趕我出，無處著身。汝能帶取我行，便當嫁汝。」
  程曰：「我自有妻，會服事吾母，如何無故舍之而别娶？且與汝同行，路上人必喚姦盜，於事不穩便。」而婦曰：「如此時，汝自先去。我經此路甚熟，候晚宿泊處當往相尋討。」程無詞以拒，漫應曰：「好。」遂獨行。暮抵旅店，則婦已在房内矣。
  力邀其寢。程初不肯從，慍曰：「我便走投都保，說汝掠我來強姦我。」程頗懼，又覩其容色勝厥妻，乃遂其請。將曉復云：「我後須到汝家。若怕妻不容，自向近處别討一住屋，但時時過來相就亦得。」臨别，於手帕内取一衲襖與程。
  程既還家，不以語母妻。所居五里外有虛市曰廣平，距邑十五里，程日一往。忽被疾，始與母道所見，曰：「彼婦恰入房相存問，坐床上移時，方出門去。」母駭愕，蓋畧無形影也。迨曉而死。母殮以衲襖而葬之。
  妻後更適人，惟生一女，及嫁，程母往依，遂空一室。

Cheng Fa, a man of Fuliang, worked for hire and was constantly back and forth between the Jiang and the Zhe country.

In the ninth month of the fourteenth year of the Chunxi era, coming home from Lin'an and crossing the borders of Qian county, he met a woman on the road in the clear early morning. Her face was haggard with trouble.

"I am unlucky," she said to him. "My husband is a cruel man and I am always being beaten; and his mother is fiercer still. I have not had one settled day. Last night they turned me out of the house and I have nowhere to put myself. If you will take me with you, I will marry you."

"I have a wife of my own," said Cheng, "and she looks after my mother. How am I to put her aside for nothing and marry somebody else? And if we two travel together, everyone on the road will call us fornicators and thieves. Nothing good will come of it."

"Then go on ahead," said the woman. "I know this road very well. I will come and find you at your lodging this evening."

Cheng had no argument left, and answered carelessly: "Very well." And he went on alone.

At dusk he came to the inn, and the woman was already in his room.

She pressed him to lie with her. At first he would not. Then she said angrily: "I will go straight to the headman and tell him you carried me off and forced me."

Cheng was very much afraid. And he saw that she was better-looking than his wife. So he did as she asked.

Towards dawn she said: "I shall have to come to your house eventually. If you are afraid your wife will not have me, take another room somewhere nearby, and I can come to you there from time to time."

At parting she took a padded coat out of a handkerchief and gave it to him.

When Cheng got home he said nothing of it to his mother or his wife. There was a market called Guangping five li from where they lived, fifteen li from the county town, and Cheng went there every day.

Then he was taken ill, and for the first time he told his mother what he had been seeing. "That woman came into the room just now to ask how I was," he said. "She sat on the bed for a while and then went out of the door."

His mother was aghast. There was nothing there at all.

He died at daybreak. His mother dressed him in the padded coat and buried him in it.

His wife afterwards married again. She had borne one daughter. When the daughter married, Cheng's mother went to live with her, and the room was left empty.
