notes:
  - term: "Cold is the diagnostic"
    text: "In Chinese ghost fiction, cold is not atmosphere — it is evidence. A ghost is yin concentrated, and the first thing a living body notices is a drop in temperature. Yuan Mei makes it measurable: walnut, plate, half the mat, into the bone. It is an unusually physical attempt to describe what a ghost actually does to you."
  - term: "Breath as the weapon"
    text: "The skull doesn't touch him for most of the scene; it *breathes* on him through the matting (张口隔席吹我). Qi — breath — is the medium by which yin and yang act on each other, which is why the attack is also a description: the skull is exhaling its own condition into him, and it spreads exactly as a cold draught would."
  - term: "A skull, not a skeleton"
    text: "骷髅 is the bare head. In Chinese lore the skull retains the last of the dead person's yin long after the rest of the body has gone, which is why skulls are a recurring nuisance rather than a symbol. This one is not haunting anyone for a reason — it is simply doing what skulls do, and the man got in the way."
  - term: "Why nobody digs"
    text: "The four friends all want to dig the thing up; Min's son refuses, because of consequences, and the room is locked instead. Disturbing a grave was a real criminal offence under Qing law, and uncovering bones would have exposed a household to prosecution. Yuan Mei records the social fact in half a clause and moves on."

Min Maojia of Hangzhou loved chess. His teacher, a man named Sun, often played with him.

In the sixth month of the fifth year of the Yongzheng reign it was very hot. Min asked five friends over and they played a round-robin. When Sun had finished his game he said, "I'm tired. I'll sleep a while in the east wing and come back and settle this."

Presently there was a screaming from the east wing. Min and the other four ran. Sun was face down on the floor, slaver all over his jaw.

They got ginger juice into him and he came round, and they asked him.

"I was lying on the bed, not properly asleep, when I felt a cold spot on my back about the size of a walnut. It grew to the size of a plate. Then half the mat was cold, and the cold went into my bones. I couldn't think what it was.

"Then there was a puffing under the bed. I looked down — and a skull was blowing at me through the matting. I fainted with fright and fell on the floor. The skull then butted me with its head. When it heard you coming it left."

All four of them wanted to dig.

Min's son was afraid of the consequences and would not. So the east wing was locked up.
