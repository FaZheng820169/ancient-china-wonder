  至一石洞，直可丈许，高极数十寻，两壁光明如烛，有老憎垂眉独坐。江趋谒之，老僧略一点首，闭目入定。汪倚壁而俟，见老僧顶上圆光忽起，现一人金盔铁甲，手横丈八矛，上悬小首级累累无算。正惊愕间，盔顶上现一黄犬，屈后足作人跪，骈前足作合掌状，宛如礼佛。久之，犬倦伏。犬顶上现一宰官，象简绯袍，峨冠博带，两袖出金银摩开，似有喜色。亡何，宰官顶上，又现出一女于，描眉画目，绝非良家妇。解杏缬衫，露逍遥服，右手执拂，左手握牟尼一声，取蒲团铺宰官顶上，端然趺坐。而女子顶上，又现出一婴孩，瑶环瑜珥，类仕族佳儿。婴孩顶上，划然一声，现一人，头童齿豁，与老僧面目酷肖。累肩叠迹，如七级浮屠，层层矗立。汪仰面凝视。半炊许，与老僧酤肖者，渐缩如豆，堕入婴孩顶穴，婴孩一斤斗，翻落女子道冠，悄然而灭。女子执拂起，揭蒲团向宰官当头一击，盬其脑，如蜂投穴。宰官急嗾其犬，犬以头抵触，宰官三摩其顶，伸脚忽下。犬人立而蹄端堕武将兜鍪，扼其首，亦侧身而入。武将怒发，持矛筑僧顶，呀然而豁，凿坯竟遁。珥上圆光，一时尽敛。老僧瞪目笑曰：“定中魔扰，又历千年浩劫矣！”
  汪具述所见。老僧曰：“此吾夙世因。吾第一世为武安君白起。伊阙之战，斩首二十四万，破赵长平，取四十万人尽杀之，复坑降卒不下数万。阎摩王大怒，转轮回六道，受诸怖苦。至唐时，始与李林甫同日托生。彼为牛，吾为犬。因念前生业报，虽堕畜生道中，一心皈佛。阎摩王喜，仍现宰官身，得度生宋时为贾似道。朝衣一着，迷失本来；起多宝阁，广通贿赂，贻误国家；木棉庵被杀后，投入阴曹。复大怒曰：“贪吏求金，何异娼家爱钞，罚作妓！”生明季时，为卞玉京。后得高僧慧指，洗心改行，为女道士十七年。花粉劫中，一朝觉悟，许转男身。又因生前不洁，于夭殇道中光转一关，生江东顾户部家，名阿绶，七岁而殇。今始度入佛门，虔修善果。循环数世，如影随行，勿谓五衍车边，漫作天魔游戏也！”
  汪大骇异，别老僧下山。告诸密友，重往迹之，而石磴云封，竟迷其处。

In his youth, Wang Kuipu went with two or three close friends to see Huangshan. They climbed by creepers and vines as far as the middle of the mountain, where the slanting sun was about to drop and the dusk came on grey, and his friends would go no further. Wang, in a fit of pique, climbed on alone. A few dozen paces on, the sky went suddenly black: the moon smothered in heavy cloud, thunder driving a hard rain. By the flicker of lightning he hunted for the path upward.

He came to a stone cave, some ten zhang deep, several tens of xun high, its two walls bright as though lit by candles. An old monk with drooping brows sat alone in it. Wang hurried forward to pay his respects; the monk inclined his head slightly, shut his eyes, and entered contemplation. Wang set himself against the wall to wait.

Then he saw the halo on the monk's crown rise — and within it a figure appear: gold helmet, iron mail, a long spear held crosswise, hung with innumerable small severed heads. Even as he marvelled, a yellow dog appeared on the crown of the helmet, its hind legs folded as a man kneels, its forelegs pressed together as if in salutation, wholly like a thing at prayer to the Buddha. After a long while the dog lay down tired.

On the dog's head appeared an official — ivory tablet, crimson robe, high cap, broad sash — and out of both his sleeves gold and silver came pouring, and he seemed pleased. Presently on the official's head a woman appeared: painted brows, painted eyes, by no means a decent householder's wife. She loosened her apricot-dyed jacket, showing a loose robe beneath, took a whisk in her right hand and a rosary in her left, and spreading a meditation cushion on the official's head sat down cross-legged and straight.

On the woman's head there appeared a baby, with jade rings and gem ear-plugs, like a fine child of an official house. And on the baby's head, with a rending sound, there appeared a man, bald and gap-toothed, whose face closely resembled the old monk's.

Shoulder on shoulder, footprint on footprint, like a pagoda of seven storeys, they stood layer upon layer. Wang gazed up. After half a cooking-pot of time, the one who so resembled the monk shrank to the size of a bean and dropped into the baby's fontanel; the baby turned a somersault, fell into the woman's Daoist crown, and quietly vanished. The woman rose with her whisk, lifted the cushion and struck the official full on the head, boring into his brain like a bee into a hole. The official at once set his dog on; the dog butted with its head; the official stroked its crown three times, stretched out a foot, and went down. The dog rose on two legs, and its feet came down on the general's helmet; it seized his head and went in sideways too. The general's hair stood up in rage; he took his spear and drove it at the monk's crown, which opened with a cry, and he bored through and was gone. The halo above the crown folded away entirely.

The old monk opened his eyes and smiled.

"Demons troubling me in contemplation. Another kalpa of a thousand years gone through."

Wang described all he had seen.

The old monk said: "This is my own cause from former lives. In my first life I was Bai Qi, Lord of Wu'an. At the battle of Yique I beheaded two hundred and forty thousand; breaking Zhao at Changping I took four hundred thousand and killed them every one, and buried the surrendered soldiers besides — tens of thousands more. King Yama was greatly angered and turned me through the six paths of rebirth, to suffer every terror. By Tang times I was at last born again on the same day as Li Linfu — he as an ox, I as a dog. Because I bore in mind the retribution of my former life, even fallen into the path of animals I turned my whole heart to the Buddha. Yama was pleased and let me appear again in an official's body; and so in the Song I was saved into the life of Jia Sidao.

"But once I had put on the court robe, I lost my original nature. I raised the Pavilion of Many Treasures, took bribes far and wide, and ruined the state. After I was killed at Mummian Hermitage I was flung back into the courts of the dead, and the King said in great anger: *A greedy officer who hunts for gold — how is he different from a harlot who loves cash? Let him be punished by being made one.* And in the late Ming I was Bian Yujing.

"Afterwards I met the wise finger of a high monk, washed my heart and changed my ways, and was a woman of the Dao for seventeen years. In the kalpa of powder and pollen I awoke one morning, and was granted leave to turn into a male body. But because I had been unclean in life, I had to pass one more gate on the path of those who die young: I was born into the Gu household of Jiangdong, a family of the Revenue Ministry, named Ashou, and died at seven. Only now have I crossed into the gate of the Buddha, to cultivate good fruit in earnest.

"To circle so through several generations, following a man like his shadow — do not mistake it for a game of heavenly demons played beside the five-vehicle cart."

Wang was amazed and appalled. He took leave of the monk and went down the mountain. He told his close friends, and they went back together to look for the place — but the stone steps were sealed in cloud, and they lost it entirely.

*The Duo comment: The scholar in the goose-cage — the thing is strange enough, but what has it to do with waking the world? This is surely the sixteen-foot golden body showing itself, making a transformation-picture of the eighteen layers of hell, to preach the bodhi-law of the city of Gaya to a good man.*
