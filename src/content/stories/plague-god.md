notes:
  - term: "The Plague God"
    text: "In Chinese popular religion an epidemic is not a natural event but a visit from an office-holder. The standard form is 五瘟使者, the Five Plague Messengers, who have a route and a schedule. You do not cure plague so much as see the god off — which is why the sick here are told exactly what to do."
  - term: "Hospitality as a binding obligation"
    text: "The mechanism is precise: Su showed respect before the god knew what it was dealing with, and the debt stands. It outranks the god's own function. Compare 煞神受枷 in the same collection, where a death-god is defeated because it stopped to eat food laid out for the dead."
  - term: "The revenge becomes a job"
    text: "The joke is structural: the plague-god's gratitude ruins Su's year anyway. He is not spared, he is employed — dragged from house to house, worn out with food and drink, while not one person in his household falls ill. Yuan Mei at his most purely comic."
  - term: "Irreverence as the winning move"
    text: "This is one of very few Chinese tales where cheek wins. Everywhere else in the collection the correct response to a god is courage (叶老脱), procedure (酆都知县), or correct ritual (鬼有三技). Here it is mockery followed by courtesy, and it works because the courtesy is real."

Su Danlao of Hangzhou was a joker and a great mocker, and people disliked him for it.

One New Year's day somebody painted the Plague God on paper and nailed it over his door.

Danlao came out in the morning, opened the door, saw it, and laughed. He brought the Plague God in, gave it the seat of honour, drank with it, and then burned it.

That year there was a great plague. Every family with someone sick sacrificed to the Plague God.

And in each house the sick person spoke in the god's voice:

"On New Year's day I was treated with courtesy by Su Danlao, and I am ashamed to have nothing to give him back. If you want me to lift this, you must invite Mr Su to keep me company. Only then will I go."

So the families sacrificing to the Plague God competed with one another to invite Su, and Su spent every day rushing about, worn out with food and drink.

Of the dozen or so people in his household, not one fell ill.
