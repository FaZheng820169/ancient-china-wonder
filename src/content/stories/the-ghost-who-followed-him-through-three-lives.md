  扬州吴髯行九，盐贾子也，年二十，将往广东某藩司署中赘娶。舟至滕王阁下，白昼见一女与公差来舟中，云：「寻君三世，今日得见面矣。」吴髯茫然不知所来。家人知为冤鬼，日以苕帚打其见处，无益也。从此吴髯言语与平时迥异。由江西以及广东，二鬼皆不去。

  入赘之日，女鬼忽入洞房，索其坐位，与新人争上下，惟新人与吴髯闻其声，云「我本汉阳孀妇，与吴狎昵，遂订婚姻，以所畜万金与至苏州买屋开张布字号，订明月日来汉阳迎娶。不意吴挟金去，五年竟无消息。我因自经死，到黄泉哭诉，汉阳城隍移查苏州城隍，回批云：『此人已生湖南。』寻至湖南诉城隍，又查明已生扬州，及至扬州，而吴又来广东。追至江西，始得相逢。今日婚姻之事，我不能阻，但须同享荣华」等语。新人大骇，白之藩台。不得已，竟虚其位待之，始得安然。鬼差口索杯箸求食，乃另设席相待。

  阅一月，吴髯告归，买舟回扬，鬼亦索舆甚迫，欲随其舆以登舟。扬州士人早知此事而不信，于吴髯抵扬之日，填街塞巷，以待其归。见其四舆入城，前果二空舆，肩舆者亦觉其若有人坐。一时好事者作《再生缘》传奇。

  阅半月，吴髯妻与女鬼约修道场七日，焚冥镪于琼花观中，劝之去，女鬼欣然诺之。其时鬼差已去，道场中设女魂牌于殿之西侧，每日吴髯妻设席亲祭。至第七日，大雨，遣家人往供。家人失足跌于路，即供以泥污之馔。鬼大嚷不止。吴髯责其家人，而髯妻又约以九日道场。圆满之。故女鬼向髯妻称谢，谓吴髯曰：「后十年来，再索汝命，我且暂去。」

  髯惧，舍身为城隍役。至期，则白日睡去。至今扬之人皆知吴九胡子为活勾差。

The widow hanged herself and 到黃泉哭訴, complained weeping at the Yellow Springs, and from there the machinery turns: the City God of Hanyang 移查 — issues a formal query — to the City God of Suzhou; the reply comes back 此人已生湖南, the man has been reborn in Hunan; she goes there, files again, and the file again tracks him, 又查明已生揚州. The underworld in these tales is not a place of judgement so much as a registry with a working reincarnation index, and a private plaintiff with patience can walk an office-to-office paper chase across four provinces and three births. This is the household's whole repertoire: a broom, applied to the ground. The Chinese folk gesture of sweeping-and-striking the place of an apparition assumes the ghost is attached to a location, and the tale quietly corrects it. She is not at a place. Wu Ran of Yangzhou, ninth among his brothers, was the son of a salt merchant. At twenty he was going to Guangdong, to be married into the household of a provincial treasurer. When the boat reached the Pavilion of Prince Teng, in broad daylight he saw a woman come aboard with a government runner, and she said: "I have been looking for you through three lifetimes, and today I have finally seen your face."

Wu Ran had no idea where she had come from. His family knew she must be a wronged ghost, and every day they beat the spot where she had been seen with a broom, which did no good at all. From then on Wu Ran's speech was altogether different from his usual manner. From Jiangxi to Guangdong, neither the woman nor the runner left him.

On the day of the wedding the woman ghost walked straight into the bridal chamber and demanded her seat, contending for precedence with the bride. Only the bride and Wu Ran could hear her.

"I was a widow of Hanyang," she said. "I was intimate with Wu, and we pledged ourselves to marry. I gave him the ten thousand in silver I had saved, so that he might go to Suzhou and buy a house and open a cloth shop, and we fixed the day he would come back to Hanyang to fetch me. Instead he took the money and went, and for five years there was no word of him. I hanged myself. I went weeping to the Yellow Springs. The City God of Hanyang sent to enquire of the City God of Suzhou, and the reply came back: this man has been reborn in Hunan. I went to Hunan and complained there, and that City God found he had been reborn again, in Yangzhou; and when I reached Yangzhou, Wu had gone on to Guangdong. I followed as far as Jiangxi, and there at last I found him. Today's marriage I cannot prevent. But I must have my share of the honour and the wealth."

The bride was terrified and told the provincial treasurer. There was nothing for it but to leave a seat empty for the ghost, and only then was there any peace. The ghost's runner then began to ask for cups and chopsticks and food, and a separate place was laid for him.

After about a month Wu Ran announced that he was going home and bought passage back to Yangzhou. The ghost pressed urgently for a chair of her own, meaning to follow his chair aboard the boat. The gentry of Yangzhou had long since heard the story and did not believe it, and on the day of his return they filled the streets and lanes to watch him come in. They saw four sedan chairs enter the city. The two in front were indeed empty, though the bearers felt as if someone were sitting in them. Someone with time on his hands turned the whole thing into a play called The Bond Renewed.

A fortnight later Wu Ran's wife and the woman ghost agreed on a seven-day rite, burning paper money in the Qionghua Abbey, to persuade her to go; and the ghost gladly consented. By now the runner had gone. In the abbey they set the woman's spirit tablet up on the west side of the hall, and every day Wu Ran's wife laid a place and made the offering with her own hands. On the seventh day it rained hard, and a servant was sent out with the food. He slipped and fell in the road, and served up the offering fouled with mud. The ghost raised a great outcry that would not stop. Wu Ran scolded the servant, and his wife agreed to a second rite of nine days, and brought it to its close.

The woman ghost thanked Wu Ran's wife, and said to Wu Ran: "Ten years from now I will come for your life. For the present I take my leave."

Wu Ran was afraid. He gave himself up to serve as a runner for the City God, and when the time came he slept through the daylight hours. To this day everyone in Yangzhou knows Wu the Ninth Whiskers as a living summoner.
