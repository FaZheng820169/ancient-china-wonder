    text: "Chi You is the ancient rebel whom the Yellow Emperor defeated at Zhuolu; legend says his blood reddened the ground, and the salt lake at Xiezhou in Shanxi was identified as the place. So the god of war who fought the founding emperor is also the reason the empire's salt supply failed — a myth about the state's most important revenue stream."
  - term: "Why Guan Yu is asked"
    text: "Guan Yu was born at Xiezhou, right beside those salt pools, and is the local god of the place. He is also the deity who gets called in whenever a problem needs a general rather than a judge. The salt pools were the fiscal heart of the Chinese state for two thousand years; a failure there was a national emergency, and the local boy is the one who handles it."
  - term: "Zhou Cang and the rotten log"

Here his statue stands forever chaining a piece of rotten wood. That is what is left of Chi You's wife. Yuan Mei is reporting a thing you could walk into a temple and look at."
  - term: "The word for a smuggler"
    text: "盐枭 yánxiāo — 'salt owl', the standard term for a salt smuggler. 枭 is the owl, and by extension a ferocious outlaw (as in 枭雄, an ambitious brigand). Yuan Mei's tale supplies a literal etymology: the word comes from Chi You's wife, whose name was Xiao. It is a folk pun, and he delivers it as an explanation."

Yue Shuixuan was passing the salt pools of Puzhou in Shanxi when he noticed something odd inside the temple of Lord Guan.

There was a statue of Zhang Fei seated facing south, alongside Guan Yu himself. And beside them stood Zhou Cang, eyes glaring, one hand dragging an iron chain — and chained to nothing but a rotten piece of wood.

He asked the locals what it meant.

"That," they said, "is the salt rebel."

"In the Yuanyou reign of the Song, the water of the salt pools was drawn off and boiled for several days, and no salt would form. The merchants were terrified, and they prayed at the temple.

"That night Lord Guan appeared to them in a dream and said:

"'Your salt pool has been seized by Chi You, and that is why it will not make salt. I accept the offerings here, so it is my business to deal with it. But Chi You's soul I can bind. His wife, whose name is Xiao, is fiercer and worse than he is, and I cannot bind her. Only my brother Zhang Yide can capture her. I have sent to Yizhou for him.'

"They woke. That very day they added a statue of Zhang Fei to the temple.

"That night there was a great wind and thunder — and a rotten piece of wood was lying on the iron chain.

"The next day they drew water and boiled it, and the yield was ten times what it had been."

And that, they said, is where the word for a salt smuggler comes from.
