notes:
  - term: "A guest-star, not a falling star"
    text: "见客星飞入南斗 — he saw a guest-star fly into the Southern Dipper. 客星 (guest-star) is the technical term for a light that appears where no star belongs — a nova, a comet, a meteor. 南斗 is the Southern Dipper, six stars in Sagittarius, distinct from the familiar Northern Dipper. The observation is given by a serving official who also has the omen-book to hand, i.e. the same man is both the instrument and the reader of the instrument."
  - term: "The paces of Yu, and the hair as a substitute"
    text: "法宜剪发寸许，东西禹步三匝，便可移祸他人 — the remedy is to cut off about an inch of hair and perform the paces of Yu three times, east then west, and you may shift the calamity onto another. 禹步 is the limping gait attributed to the ancient rain-magician Yu and used in Daoist apotropaic ritual; 剪发 supplies a piece of the self to be surrendered, since hair is the only part of the body a living man can cut off and give. Note the four characters 移禍他人: the charm does not cancel anything. It relocates."
  - term: "He waved the clerks away first"
    text: "尔时我即麾去书役，依法行之 — at once I waved the clerks and runners away and performed the rite as prescribed. Han's first act is to clear the site of witnesses. The reader is told what the procedure requires, not what the operator intends, and the story leaves the two readings side by side: he cleared the ground because the rite is private, and he cleared the ground because he had decided what to do."
  - term: "The victim was his own candidate"
    text: "署中司书记者李某无故以小刀剖腹而死……李乃我荐卷门生，年少能文 — the clerk in charge of the office records, a man named Li, died for no reason at all, cutting open his own belly with a small knife; Li was a candidate whose paper I had passed, young and a good writer. 荐卷門生 is a protégé — the man owed Han his career, which is the tie that makes the substitution possible and the story nasty. The omen promised 暴亡, a sudden death; what it delivered was a suicide, which nobody can call an accident."
  - term: "Ignorance is a defence"
    text: "君辈不知天文者，虽见飞星入南斗，亦无害 — those who know no astronomy, even if they see a flying star enter the Southern Dipper, are not harmed by it. Han's answer to the obvious objection is that the omen only exists for those who can read it. This is a genuinely interesting claim — the danger resides in the interpretation, not the star — and it is also a confession that the whole of his science is a liability."
  - term: "What was the point of learning it, then"
    text: "余曰：然则公又何苦知天文，多此一事，而自祸祸人耶？韩大笑，不能答 — I said: then why did you take the trouble to learn astronomy, adding this one extra item, and bringing misfortune on yourself and on others? Han laughed heartily and could not answer. Yuan Mei delivers the punchline himself, in his own voice, as he does in a handful of the best pieces in the collection. Compare 《趙三翁》 and 《龜鑑》, where the value of divination depends on the diviner being willing to be wrong: here the expert is exactly right, and that is the disaster."

Han Qingyan, circuit intendant of Suzhou and Songjiang, understood astronomy. He once told me:

"When I was magistrate of Baoshan, in the sixth month, I was out in the fields catching locusts. I rose at the fourth watch and sat on a folding chair directing the clerks and runners, when I saw a guest-star fly into the Southern Dipper. I remembered privately what the book of prognostications says: 'He who sees this calamity will die a sudden death within a month. The remedy is to cut off about an inch of hair and perform the paces of Yu three times, east then west; then you may shift the calamity onto another.' At the time I at once waved the clerks away and did as the book prescribed. Not long after, the clerk in charge of the office records, a man named Li, died for no reason at all, cutting open his own belly with a small knife. I was unharmed. Li was a candidate whose paper I had passed; he was young and a good writer. I had not expected him to take the calamity in my place, and it grieves me to think of it."

I said to Han, in jest: "Your art of prognostication is certainly marvellous. But we who know nothing of astronomy often sit up at night and see a great many flying stars go to and fro. If one of them went into the Southern Dipper, we should not know the counter-charm at all. What would become of us?"

He said: "Those who know no astronomy — even if they see a flying star enter the Southern Dipper, it does them no harm."

I said: "Then why on earth did you take the trouble to learn astronomy, adding this one extra item, and bringing misfortune on yourself and on others?"

Han laughed heartily and could not answer.
