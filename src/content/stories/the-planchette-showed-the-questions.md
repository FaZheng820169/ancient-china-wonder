notes:
  - term: "Asking for the paper"
    text: "會試舉子求乩仙示題 — the candidates for the metropolitan examination begged the planchette spirit to show them the question. Leaking the examination paper was a capital offense in the Qing, and asking a god for it was the pious version of buying it. The premise is pure satire: men who have spent twenty years memorizing the classics to qualify as moral exemplars open their campaign for office by trying to cheat, and they ask the spirit as naturally as they would ask a broker. The spirit's cooperation makes it an accomplice; its answers make it a satirist."
  - term: "Don't know, don't know, and again don't know"
    text: "乩仙乃大書曰：不知不知又不知。眾人大笑，以仙為無知也。是科，題乃「不知命，無以為君子也」三節 — the spirit then wrote in large characters: don't know, don't know, and again don't know. The crowd roared with laughter, taking the spirit for an ignoramus. That session's question was on the three clauses ending *without knowing the Mandate, one has no way to become a gentleman*. The joke is perfectly constructed. The candidates asked for the text and were given the text — twice over, then a third time in capitals — and laughed because they could not hear it. The spirit was not ignorant; it was reading them the paper while they mocked it."
  - term: "Cannot be spoken"
    text: "仙書「不可語」三字…乃書曰：正在『不可語』上 — the spirit wrote the three characters *cannot be spoken*… and when the candidates begged without ceasing, it wrote: it lies exactly on *cannot be spoken*. The second episode escalates the game. The answer is again literal — the set passage contains the key phrase — and again the candidates read it as refusal. The spirit's hint, 'it lies exactly on cannot-be-spoken,' is as explicit as a leak can be without indicting anyone, and they still cannot parse it, because they are listening for the answer and not for the sentence."
  - term: "One character: 署"
    text: "再求仙明示之，仙書一「署」字，再叩之，則不應矣 — they begged the spirit to show them plainly; the spirit wrote the single character 署; they knocked and asked again, and it did not answer. The final hint is the cleverest and the coldest: 署, 'to sign one's name,' is the verb of the sentence they were begging for — 知之者, those who know it, sign the register of the wise; but more directly, the character points at the target passage's opening word. The spirit gives them the first brushstroke of the answer and then goes silent, like any civil servant who has leaked as much as his career allows."
  - term: "Twice in twenty-six years"
    text: "康熙戊辰…又甲午鄉試前 — in the wuchen year of Kangxi… and again before the provincial examination of the jiawu year. The two episodes are paired across twenty-six years, and the pairing is the point: this was not a one-off miracle but a recurring market — every examination season, the same pilgrimage to the planchette, the same begging, the same joke at the candidates' expense. The spirit's pattern never changes: answer truthfully, answer literally, and let them laugh. It is the examination system's own mirror, and the candidates never recognize their face in it."
  - term: "Why the spirit plays along"
    text: "眾秀才苦求不已 — the scholars begged bitterly without ceasing. The tale never says the spirit refused; it only ever complies, and its compliance is the punishment. A god who leaks papers to cheaters would be corrupt; a god who answers cheaters in riddles that are simultaneously the exact answer and a rebuke is something finer — the collection's recurring figure of the planchette as critic, the same spirit that in 《神仙不解考據》 flees a citation and in 《貧士乩仙》 humiliates a scholar. Here it does both at once: it gives them the question, and proves they cannot read."

In the wuchen year of Kangxi's reign, the candidates gathered for the metropolitan examination begged the planchette spirit to show them the question.

The spirit wrote two characters: *Don't know.*

The candidates bowed twice and begged again: "How can there be such a thing as a god who does not know?"

The spirit then wrote in large characters: *Don't know, don't know, and again don't know.*

The crowd roared with laughter, and took the spirit for an ignoramus.

That session's examination question was on the three clauses of the Analects that end: *Without knowing the Mandate, one has no way to become a gentleman.*

Again, before the provincial examination of the jiawu year, some scholars begged the planchette spirit to show them the question. The spirit wrote three characters: *Cannot be spoken.* The scholars begged bitterly and would not stop, so it wrote: *It lies exactly on "cannot be spoken."* They were more bewildered than ever, and begged the spirit to show them plainly.

The spirit wrote a single character: *to sign.*

They knocked and asked again — but it did not answer.

Before long, the question was posted: the chapter of the Analects that begins, *To know it is not so good as to love it.*
