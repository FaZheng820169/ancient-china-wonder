  李玉双孝廉家有婢，名春云，颇有姿，年十五，李欲纳为妾，与其妻有成说矣。春云白日见瓦上一男子下，拥其髻而嗅之曰：「汝发甚香，当大贵，宜从我，勿从主人。主人处馆穷儒，虽中举，不过一教官终耳。你向主人言，命其让我，且供我酒馔，我便赘汝家。」玉双闻之大怒，然亦无如何。是夜，怪竟来与婢配合。婢求主人具酒馔，如其言，则日夜安宁；否则，飞砖掷瓦之祸毕作。玉双不得已，与人谋将此屋招人承买。玉双馆于望仙桥施氏，不常在家。一日者，商人孙耕文来看屋，敲门，有苍须老翁衣灰鼠袍出迎，摇手曰：「此屋是我祖遗，并未出卖，勿听小儿玉双妄语，私相授受，将来要受讼累。」孙大骇，走告玉双，责以「父在，子不得自专。」玉双曰：「先君亡已十馀年，家中并无此翁。」乃知为怪所揶揄，冒认为父，彼此大笑。

  自后，人知屋有怪，屡卖不成。玉双乃命婢父母领女还家，勿索身价。婢剺面剪发，誓不肯归。其母虑为怪所害，以绳缚之，捆载还家，另嫁一士人。怪竟不来。

The licentiate Li Yushuang had a maidservant named Chunyun, of some beauty. She was fifteen. Li wanted to take her as a concubine, and had already come to an understanding with his wife about it.

One day in broad daylight Chunyun saw a man come down from the roof-tiles. He put his arms round her coiffure and sniffed at it: "Your hair smells very sweet. You are going to be greatly honoured. You should follow me, not your master. Your master is a poor scholar who keeps a school; even if he passes the provincial examination he will end as nothing more than a school official. Tell your master to yield you to me, and to supply me with wine and food, and I will come and live in your house as a husband."

Li was furious when he heard it, but there was nothing he could do. That night the spirit came and lay with the maid.

She begged her master to provide wine and food. When he did as she asked, the house was quiet day and night; otherwise there was a plague of bricks flying and tiles being thrown. Li had no choice but to discuss with people putting the house up for sale.

Li taught at the Shi family's house on Wangxian Bridge and was not often at home. One day a merchant named Sun Gengwen came to look at the house. He knocked, and an old man with a grizzled beard in a grey squirrel-fur robe came out to meet him, waved his hand, and said: "This house is my ancestral property and is not for sale. Do not listen to the nonsense of that boy Yushuang, or take it over privately between you; you will find yourself in a lawsuit later."

Sun was greatly alarmed, and went off and told Li, reproaching him: "While your father lives, a son may not act on his own."

Li said: "My father died more than ten years ago, and there is no such old man in the household." Then they knew the spirit had been mocking them, pretending to be his father, and the two of them laughed together.

After that people knew the house was haunted and it could not be sold. Li told the maid's parents to take their daughter home and not ask for her purchase price. The maid gashed her own face and cut off her hair, and swore she would not go home. Her mother, fearing the spirit would do her harm, tied her up with rope, bundled her home, and married her to a scholar. The spirit never came again.
