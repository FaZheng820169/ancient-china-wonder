Ten terms do most of the work in this universe. Each is explained as the stories use it, not as the philosophers define it.

**Qi (气).** The breath-stuff everything is made of: mountains, bodies, ghosts, swords. Because everything is qi in different densities, the boundary between solid and spirit is a difference of degree, not kind — which is why a fox can condense into a woman and a ghost can be dispersed by a shout. The universe is one substance, playing at many forms.

**Essence (精, jing).** The refined, concentrated form of qi — gathered from sun and moon, mountain mist, one's own body. Whatever accumulates essence gains power and eventually awareness. The law behind 'seniority is power': a thousand-year-old tree has simply been collecting longer.

**Cultivation (修炼, xiulian).** The deliberate gathering and refining of essence to change what you are. Humans cultivate toward immortality; animals toward human form. It takes centuries, requires discipline, and is policed by heaven. [The full system.](/worldview/cultivation-system)

**Tribulation (劫, jie).** The examination heaven sets when a being attempts a major promotion — thunderbolts for the thousand-year snake-spirit, ordeals of temptation for the would-be immortal. Fail, and centuries of work burn away. The universe promotes on merit, but it tests.

**Karma (因果, yinguo).** Cause and effect, kept across lifetimes in a literal ledger. Karma is the deepest law in the universe — deeper than any god's authority — and the engine of most plots: the haunting is an unpaid debt, the miracle is a settled account.

**Shen (神) and Xian (仙).** The two kinds of greater being: gods, who are *appointed* to office in the celestial bureaucracy, and immortals, who are *self-made* through cultivation. The establishment and the mountain. [The distinction in full.](/worldview/immortals-and-gods)

**Gui (鬼).** A ghost: a human soul that failed to be processed into rebirth, usually because of a violent death, a missing burial, or an unsettled account. A case file, not a monster. [What ghosts want and fear.](/worldview/ghosts)

**Yao / Jing / Guai (妖/精/怪).** The woken things: animals, plants and objects that have lived long enough to gain power and human shape. Not demons in the Western sense — beings with their own rules, morals and careers. [The taxonomy.](/worldview/monsters-and-yaoguai)

**Xia (侠).** The knight-errant: the swordsman whose loyalty is to a personal code — to the debt of bread, to his own judgment of worth — rather than to any institution. The ancestor of all wuxia. [The Tang originals.](/features/tang-swordsmen)

**Fate and ledger-keeping (禄命/簿).** Every life has its entry — lifespan, fortune, deaths — in the registers of the underworld. But the entries can be annotated: by merit, by filial piety, by an official's error. The tension between the written fate and the amended one is one of the genre's great plot machines.
