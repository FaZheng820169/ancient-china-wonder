New readers of Western fantasy arrive with a map in their heads — gods above, mortals below, monsters at the edges. The Chinese universe has one too; it is simply drawn on different lines. Here is the whole map in one page.

## The map: three floors, one law

The cosmos has three realms. **Heaven** above is a court — the Jade Emperor's celestial government, with gods as its officials. **The mortal world** in the middle is the only place where anything can *change*: humans become immortals, animals become spirits, ghosts get reborn. **The underworld** below is a processing centre: ten courts of magistrate-kings who judge every soul, settle its karmic account, and send it back up to be reborn. [Read more about the architecture.](/worldview/three-realms)

Between and around the three floors lie the sacred addresses: **Kunlun**, the western mountain where the Queen Mother of the West grows the peaches of immortality, and **Penglai**, the drifting eastern isles of the immortals. Think of them as the two poles where the worlds touch.

## The cast: who you will meet

- **Gods (神)** — appointed officials of the invisible government: dragon-kings of rivers and seas, city gods, the god of thunder. Powerful, jurisdictional, and as fallible as any bureaucracy.
- **Immortals (仙)** — self-made deathless ones: humans who cultivated themselves out of mortality. They hold no office, live on mountains, and behave like magnificent dropouts. [Lü Dongbin](/worldview/lu-dongbin) is the beloved archetype.
- **Ghosts (鬼)** — the dead who failed to move on: wronged, unburied, or bound by an unsettled debt. Most hauntings are case files waiting to be closed.
- **Fox spirits (狐)** — the great shape-shifters: animals cultivated into human form, by turns predatory and the most faithful lovers in the library.
- **Sprites and strange beasts (精怪/异兽)** — everything else that wakes up, from peony-spirits to the [creatures of the Shan Hai Jing](/features/shan-hai-jing-creatures).

## The rules: five things that are always true

1. **Seniority is power.** Age accumulates essence. Respect anything a thousand years old.
2. **Everything runs on rules.** Every spirit has a condition, a weakness, a tell. Stories are won by learning them.
3. **Karma keeps the ledger.** Deeds compound across lifetimes. No one — mortal, spirit or god — is exempt from the accounting.
4. **The boundary is porous.** Humans marry ghosts and foxes; immortals walk the roads; dragon princesses herd sheep. The drama is in the crossing.
5. **Morality outranks species.** In this tradition the question is never 'is it human?' but 'does it behave with ren — human-heartedness?' Foxes pass; kings fail.

## Your first three stories

Start with [Song Dingbo Catches a Ghost](/stories/song-dingbo) — four hundred words and the funniest manual of ghost-rules ever written. Then [Li Ji Slays the Serpent](/stories/li-ji), the first great monster-slaying, won by a thirteen-year-old with a plan. Then [Nie Xiaoqian](/stories/nie-xiaoqian), the genre at full power: a temple, a ghost, a demon, a swordsman, and a redemption.

After that, the [Worldview](/worldview) section will take you as deep as you want to go.
