**Zhiguai (志怪)** means, literally, 'records of the strange' — and it is the oldest continuous fiction genre in the world. For roughly two thousand years, Chinese writers have been collecting, polishing and inventing short accounts of marvels: ghosts, fox-spirits, immortals, monsters, resurrections, dreams, miracles and uncanny coincidences. This website is a library of that tradition.

## A genre of notebooks

The first thing to grasp is that zhiguai was not written as 'fiction' in the modern sense. It was written as **reportage**. Gan Bao, the fourth-century historian whose *Soushen Ji* (In Search of the Supernatural) is the founding classic, presented his ghost stories the way he presented history: as gathered testimony about things that had, reportedly, happened. A zhiguai entry often names the place, the year, the source — *this was told to me by a man of Runan* — and the plainness is deliberate. The marvellous is most marvellous when entered in a clerk's handwriting.

This gives the genre its distinctive texture. A zhiguai tale is usually **short** — sometimes three lines, rarely more than a few pages. It is concrete: names, roads, prices, dates. And it does not explain itself. The modern reader should expect endings that land like a door closing, and marvels offered without apology.

## What lives in it

The population of zhiguai is the population of the universe this site maps: [gods and immortals](/worldview/immortals-and-gods), [ghosts](/worldview/ghosts) with unfinished business, [fox-spirits](/worldview/fox-spirits) cultivating themselves into human shape, [woken trees and stones and household objects](/worldview/monsters-and-yaoguai), [dragon-kings](/worldview/dragons) ruling the waters, and the vast filing system of [the Courts of the Dead](/worldview/underworld). A handful of rules govern everything: seniority is power; spirits run on learnable rules; karma keeps accounts; and the boundary between the human and non-human is permanently under negotiation.

## The family tree

Zhiguai is the trunk from which nearly all Chinese fiction branches. The Tang dynasty turned the plain 'record' into the polished **chuanqi** — longer, plotted, romantic tales like *Liu Yi* and *Nie Yinniang*, where Chinese fiction became self-conscious art. The Song and Ming printed the great casebooks. The tradition's acknowledged summit came in the late seventeenth century with Pu Songling's **Liaozhai Zhiyi**, nearly five hundred tales in which the genre learned to be tender, satirical and heartbroken all at once; the eighteenth century added Yuan Mei's *Zi Bu Yu* and Ji Yun's *Yuewei Caotang Biji*. And today the lineage runs straight into Chinese film, television, and the entire 'cultivation' genre of web-fiction — which is, at bottom, zhiguai with the brakes off.

## How to read it

Three habits will serve you. First: **read short tales slowly** — the three-line entries carry their meaning in implication, like the myths of the Shan Hai Jing. Second: **trust the rules** — every marvel has a logic, and the pleasure of the genre is learning to reason inside it. Third: **watch for the moral accounting** — in this tradition the supernatural is never arbitrary; it is how the universe settles its books.
