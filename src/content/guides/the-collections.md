Chinese strange fiction does not arrive in novels. It arrives in **notebooks**: a scholar keeps a running record of marvels for thirty years, and what survives is a pile of short entries with no beginning and no end. The unit is not the book; it is the *collection*.

That matters for reading. A collection has a temperature. Some are devout, some are comic, some are angry, some are doing taxonomy with a straight face. Knowing which notebook a tale came out of tells you how to read it before you start. Here are the collections that supply this library, oldest first, with the tales we have taken from each.

You can also filter the [full library](/stories) by source, dynasty or tag.

## 1. Shan Hai Jing — the bestiary-geography (《山海经》)

**Compiled over centuries, from the Warring States period onward. No single author.**

Not a story collection at all: a catalogue of mountains, rivers, tribes, gods and creatures, written as field notes. There is no plot and very rarely a character. *West three hundred li, there is a mountain; on its south slope much jade, on its north much copper; there is a bird there shaped like an owl with a human face, and where it appears there is great drought.*

Everything later draws on it: the geography, the monsters, the Queen Mother of the West, the bird that fills the sea. It is the bedrock layer of the universe and the reason Chinese fantasy has a bestiary before it has a plot.

**Here:** [Jingwei Fills the Sea](/stories/jingwei) · [Kuafu Chases the Sun](/stories/kuafu)

## 2. Soushen Ji — the founding collection (《搜神记》)

**Gan Bao. Eastern Jin, 4th century.**

The book that establishes zhiguai as a genre, and it was written by a **historian**. Gan Bao presented his ghost stories the way he presented history: as gathered testimony, with places, dates and informants. That plainness is the whole trick. The marvellous is most marvellous when entered in a clerk's handwriting.

It also fixes a great many of the patterns the later tradition just inherits: the ghost you can outwit by asking what it is afraid of, the animal that marries a human, the unjustly executed woman whose blood turns the sky, the child who repays a cruelty with a sword.

**Here:** [Li Ji Slays the Serpent](/stories/li-ji) · [Song Dingbo Catches a Ghost](/stories/song-dingbo) · [The Two Swords](/stories/gan-jiang-mo-ye) · [Dong Yong and the Weaver Maid](/stories/dong-yong) · [The Husband-and-Wife Tree](/stories/han-ping) · [The Filial Wife of Donghai](/stories/donghai-xiaofu)

Two near neighbours belong with it: 《搜神后记》 ([The Snail-Shell Girl](/stories/baishui-sunu)) and 《列异传》 ([Tan Sheng and the Ghost Wife](/stories/tan-sheng)). A third, 《续齐谐记》 (Wu Jun, 6th century), gives us [The Goose-Cage Scholar](/stories/yang-xian-shu-sheng) — a man inside a man inside a man, and the most influential trick-ending in East Asian fiction.

## 3. You Ming Lu — the Buddhist layer (《幽明录》)

**Attributed to Liu Yiqing. Liu Song, 5th century.**

The hidden and the visible worlds, interleaved. This is where the older folklore starts absorbing Buddhist machinery — rebirth, karmic cause, otherworldly jurisdictions — and where the great time-slip stories get their shape. The collection is also the home of the most quietly brutal endings in the tradition: people who get exactly what they asked for.

**Here:** [The Peach-Blossom Spring of Mount Tiantai](/stories/liu-chen-ruan-zhao) · [The Girl Who Sold Face Powder](/stories/mai-hu-fen-nu-zi)

## 4. The Tang chuanqi — literature arrives (唐传奇)

**8th–9th centuries. Named authors: Shen Jiji, Li Gongzuo, Li Fuyan, Pei Xing, Chen Xuanyou, Li Chaowei, Du Guangting, Yuan Jiao.**

A completely different animal. *Chuanqi* — 'transmissions of the marvellous' — are long, polished, self-conscious works of prose fiction written by examination candidates for each other. Characters have interiority; scenes are set; the ending is designed. This is where Chinese prose fiction learns to be a novel's length.

The Tang tales give this library its dream-machinery, its swordsmen and its fox-wives. Most of them survive because a Song imperial anthology, **Taiping Guangji** (《太平广记》, *Extensive Records of the Taiping Era*, 978), copied them in wholesale — which is why several of the entries below are cited as 'preserved in Taiping Guangji'.

**Here:** [The World Inside a Pillow](/stories/zhen-zhong) · [The Governor of South Bough](/stories/nan-ke) · [The Soul That Left](/stories/li-hun-ji) · [Miss Ren](/stories/ren-shi) · [Liu Yi and the Dragon Princess](/stories/liu-yi) · [Du Zichun and the Furnace](/stories/du-zi-chun) · [The Marriage Shop](/stories/ding-hun-dian) · [Nie Yinniang](/stories/nie-yinniang) · [Red Thread](/stories/hong-xian) · [The Curly-Bearded Stranger](/stories/qiu-ran-ke) · [The Kunlun Slave](/stories/kun-lun-nu) · [The Ancient Mirror](/stories/gu-jing-ji) · [The White Ape](/stories/bai-yuan-zhuan)

## 5. Yijian Zhi — the greatest notebook (《夷坚志》)

**Hong Mai. Southern Song, 12th century. Originally 420 juan, roughly 2,700 items; about half survives.**

Enormous, and almost unread outside China, which is a scandal. Hong Mai collected for sixty years and did something no one else did at scale: he **named his informants**. *This was told me by the vice-prefect of Changzhou, who had it from his mother's brother* — and then the story. It is reportage, and the result is a snapshot of how ordinary Song people actually imagined the supernatural.

Because the Song state ran on paperwork, the Song underworld runs on paperwork too: offices, registers, quotas, transfers, audits. If you want the machinery of [the Courts of the Dead](/worldview/underworld) at full detail, it is here rather than anywhere else.

**Here (60 tales):** [The Warrant That Sank the Boat](/stories/the-warrant-that-sank-the-boat) · [The Woman With the Cat](/stories/the-woman-with-the-cat) · [The Wife Who Died Every Afternoon](/stories/the-wife-who-died-every-afternoon) · [The Taoist Who Came to Supper](/stories/the-taoist-who-came-to-supper) · [The Hundred Meat Pies](/stories/the-hundred-meat-pies) · [The Log in the Bed](/stories/the-log-in-the-bed) · [The Bamboo That Gathered Treasure](/stories/the-bamboo-that-gathered-treasure) · [The Crab With the Dipper on Its Back](/stories/the-crab-with-the-dipper) · [A Lifetime's Salary](/stories/a-lifetimes-salary) · [The Wizard Who Could Somersault](/stories/the-wizard-who-could-somersault) · [The Ghost-Seer of Genshan Gate](/stories/the-ghost-seer) · [The Tiger at the Fireside](/stories/the-tiger-at-the-fireside) · [The Lay Brother Who Became a Pig](/stories/the-lay-brother-who-became-a-pig) · [The Clerk Who Died Twice](/stories/the-clerk-who-died-twice) · [The Wet Nurse's Relics](/stories/the-wet-nurses-relics) · [The Candle-Flowers](/stories/the-candle-flowers) · [The Woman of the Black Pine Valley](/stories/the-woman-of-the-black-pine-valley) · [The Fifth Young Lord](/stories/the-fifth-young-lord) · [The House in the Cypress Wood](/stories/the-house-in-the-cypress-wood) · [Paper and Brush in the Coffin](/stories/paper-and-brush-in-the-coffin) · [Stabbing the Spring](/stories/stabbing-the-spring) · [The Wildcat Wife](/stories/the-wildcat-wife) · [The Wine Pressed From Bone Marrow](/stories/the-wine-pressed-from-bone-marrow) · and thirty-seven more

If you read only one thing here, read [The Warrant That Sank the Boat](/stories/the-warrant-that-sank-the-boat). It is the Song underworld in a single scene: a summons performed correctly, a culprit produced, a defence of paperwork, and a ritual master with no statute left to stand on. Then read [Paper and Brush in the Coffin](/stories/paper-and-brush-in-the-coffin) for the other half of the same idea: a beaten clerk forbids his family to sue, asks for writing materials in his coffin instead, and comes back down the road at noon with two officers behind him. And read [The Candle-Flowers](/stories/the-candle-flowers) for the one place in the whole collection where Hong Mai puts himself into the story — it is 1145, he has just walked out of the hardest examination in the empire, and he loses an argument about an omen with a courtesan who turns out to be right.

## 6. Liaozhai Zhiyi — the summit (《聊斋志异》)

**Pu Songling. Qing, late 17th century. Some 490 pieces.**

The one everybody has heard of, and it earns it. A failed examination candidate in Shandong wrote near-perfect classical short stories about foxes, ghosts, flowers and examiners, and quietly used them to demolish the examination system that had ruined him. The prose is fast, the characterisation is real, and the cruelty of the world is never softened.

What Pu adds that nobody before him quite had: the supernatural as **ordinary life**. A fox-woman nags, grieves, keeps house and ages. A ghost is a person with a problem. Read [Nie Xiaoqian](/stories/nie-xiaoqian) and note that the horror and the domesticity are in the same room.

**Here (12 tales):** [Nie Xiaoqian](/stories/nie-xiaoqian) · [The Painted Skin](/stories/huapi) · [Xi Fangping Sues the Underworld](/stories/xi-fangping) · [The Taoist of Mount Lao](/stories/laoshan) · [Yingning](/stories/yingning) · [Judge Lu](/stories/lu-pan) · [The Examination for City God](/stories/kao-chenghuang) · [The Peony Spirit](/stories/xiangyu) · [The Cricket](/stories/cuizhi) · [Wang Liulang](/stories/wang-liulang) · [Abao](/stories/a-bao) · [Jiaona](/stories/jiaona)

## 7. Zibuyu — the comedy (《子不语》 and 《续子不语》)

**Yuan Mei. Qing, 18th century. Two collections.**

The title is from the *Analects*: the things the Master did not discuss. Yuan Mei was the greatest wit of his century and a deliberate heretic, and his collection is the funniest in the tradition — and the most skeptical. He reports wonders constantly and takes the wonder-business apart in the same breath: in [The Magistrate Who Went Down the Well](/stories/fengdu-magistrate), the presiding judge of the underworld denounces the priests who use gods to ruin families.

This is the source for most of the hard rules of the universe as we have systematised them — [how souls move on](/worldview/how-souls-move-on), [how a god gets a post](/worldview/divine-careers), [the substitute](/stories/li-tongpan), [the quota on profit](/stories/seizing-surplus-ghost). Yuan Mei likes rules, then likes to show you who exploits them.

**Here (72 tales across both collections):** [The Ghost's Three Tricks](/stories/ghost-three-tricks) · [The Five One-Eyes](/stories/one-eyed-five) · [The Debt of Seven Ounces](/stories/debt-of-silver) · [Two Excellent Ways to Govern Ghosts](/stories/governing-ghosts-two-ways) · [The Monster Feared Reason](/stories/monster-feared-reason) · [The Jar That Held a Soul](/stories/soul-jar) · [The Fox Licentiate](/stories/fox-licentiate) · [Climbing the Sunbeam](/stories/climbing-sunbeam) · [The True Judge Bao and the False](/stories/the-true-judge-bao-and-the-false) · [The Case of the Bound Feet](/stories/the-footbinding-case) · [The Judge Answers Questions](/stories/the-judge-answers-questions) · [The Ghost Who Sued an Actor](/stories/the-ghost-who-sued-an-actor) · [The God Nobody Could Name](/stories/the-god-nobody-could-name) · [When the Stones Spoke](/stories/when-the-stones-spoke) · [The Coffin He Rode Home](/stories/the-coffin-he-rode-home) · [The Ghost Sealed in the Prison](/stories/the-ghost-sealed-in-the-prison) · [The Corpse Who Rose for Silver](/stories/the-corpse-who-rose-for-silver) · [The Man Who Remembered His First Wife](/stories/the-man-who-remembered-his-first-wife) · [Where Ghosts Do Not Call](/stories/where-ghosts-do-not-call) · [The Voice in the Bamboo Tube](/stories/the-voice-in-the-bamboo-tube) · and fifty-two more

Two to begin with. [The Ghost Who Sued an Actor](/stories/the-ghost-who-sued-an-actor) is Yuan Mei at his most ingenious: a murdered man files his plaint with an actor playing Bao Zheng, and the magistrate's solution is to send the actor back on in costume and have the ghost follow him to the yamen. And [The Judge Answers Questions](/stories/the-judge-answers-questions) is the single clearest statement in the whole library of how the supernatural bureaucracy actually works: there is no register for you unless you are in a case.

## 8. Xieduo — the satire (《谐铎》)

**Shen Qifeng. Qing, c. 1790.**

Our largest single source, and the one that best explains the shape of this site. Shen writes short, elegant, vicious satires and closes each with a **铎曰** — a 'bell' comment, a moral delivered with an audible smirk. He is a moralist who is always in on the joke, and he reports his own most dangerous material with a disclaimer attached.

Where Yuan Mei is fascinated by how the universe works, Shen is interested in what the universe says about *us*: the corrupt examiner, the fraudulent geomancer, the henpecked magistrate, the man who buys an underworld post. Several of the [worldview](/worldview) entries exist mainly because Xieduo keeps the same machinery visible from six different angles.

**Here (60 tales):** [The Commandery Where a Day Is a Year](/stories/a-day-is-a-year) · [The Enrolment of the Ghosts](/stories/enrolment-of-ghosts) · [The Wheel of Faces](/stories/the-face-wheel) · [Mengpo Village](/stories/mengpo-village) · [The Body Outside the Body](/stories/the-body-outside) · [The Mirror That Read Hearts](/stories/the-mirror-that-reads-hearts) · [The Merman Who Cried Pearls](/stories/the-merman-servant) · [The Household in a Dream](/stories/the-household-in-a-dream) · [The Halo of Seven Lives](/stories/the-halo-of-seven-lives) · [One Cash](/stories/one-cash) · and fifty more

## 9. Yuewei Caotang Biji — the rival (《阅微草堂笔记》)

**Ji Yun. Qing, 18th century.**

Written as a deliberate rebuke to Liaozhai. Ji Yun thought Pu Songling's stories were too good to be credible, and so he wrote dry, brief, anecdotal notes in the older style — and complained about ghosts who behaved like novel characters. It is the conservative counter-argument inside the genre, and it is full of first-rate material anyway.

**Here:** [The Ghosts Who Avoided Jiang Sanmang](/stories/gui-bi-jiang-san-mang)

## Why the coverage is uneven

The library draws most heavily on three collections — Xieduo, Yijian Zhi and Zibuyu — and that is a sourcing decision rather than a judgement of quality: those three are the collections we can obtain in reliable original text and work through systematically, and we keep them deliberately equal in weight so that no single author's habits come to stand for the whole tradition. Others — Yuewei Caotang Biji above all — are still to come. Every tale on this site is translated **from the Chinese original only**, never from an existing English version, so a collection enters the library only when we can put the original characters beside the rendering.

If you want single-author immersion, [Zibuyu](/stories?source=子不语) and [Xieduo](/stories?source=谐铎) are the two deepest wells. If you want the universe's rules rather than its jokes, start with the [worldview](/worldview).
