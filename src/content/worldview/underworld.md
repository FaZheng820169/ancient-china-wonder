Every soul in the Chinese universe passes, after death, through the **Difu (地府)** — the Earth Administration — and the first thing to understand about it is that it is an administration.

Its capital is **Fengdu**, the Dark Capital. Its ruler is the Jade Emperor's counterpart below: the Great Emperor of Fengdu, and under him the **Ten Kings**, each the magistrate of a court through which the dead are processed in sequence. The newly dead arrive escorted by constables — the ox-headed and horse-faced bailiffs — and are judged from a **ledger** in which every deed of their life is recorded. The virtuous pass quickly onward to rebirth; the wicked are sent down into the **earth prisons (diyu)**, a graded system of torments matched to specific sins — the liars have their tongues pulled, the corrupt officials are crushed, the unfilial are sawn — until the debt is paid. Then they too pass on.

Two landmarks frame the exit. The dead must cross the **River of the Dead** on the Bridge of Helplessness (奈何桥, *Naihe Qiao* — the 'what-can-one-do' bridge), where the virtuous cross safely and the guilty fall to the serpents below. And on the far side waits **Meng Po**, the Old Lady Meng, who serves each soul a bowl of her **broth of forgetfulness**. Drink it, and the life you have just lived — and the judgment you have just received — is wiped clean. Then you are reborn, and begin again, ignorant.

This system explains the ghosts. A ghost is, in effect, a backlog case: a soul that for some reason — a violent death, an unburied body, an unsettled debt, a grief too strong to drink away — failed to be processed, and lingers in the mortal realm off the register. That is why so many zhiguai ghosts are solved by *paperwork*: a proper burial, a debt repaid, a wrong righted. The underworld wants them back. Its clerks are always behind.

It also explains the genre's distinctive tone. Judgment in this cosmos is not wrathful but administrative — which means it can be appealed, negotiated, bribed and occasionally sued, and the stories do all four.
