Underneath the marvels, this universe runs on a coherent set of rules for how one kind of being becomes another. Later fantasy readers will find the system oddly familiar — Chinese web-novels made it famous — but its foundations are two thousand years old, and the classical stories all assume it.

**1. Essence accumulates.** Everything is made of qi, and the refined essences — of sun and moon, of mountains and rivers, of one's own body — can be *gathered and kept*. Whatever gathers enough, gains power and eventually consciousness. This is why old things are dangerous: a thousand-year fox, a thousand-year tree, a thousand-year sword have simply been collecting longer than you.

**2. Humans cultivate toward immortality.** The human path is called *xiulian* (修炼): meditation and breath-work to refine the body's essence; diet and alchemy (the elixirs and cinnabar pills of the Taoist masters); the guarding of one's vital energies; and above all the loosening of attachment to the world — the lesson of the Handan pillow. Practised long and purely enough, the body is transmuted: the mortal husk is shed, and the cultivator becomes a *xian*, an immortal, light enough to ride the clouds.

**3. Animals cultivate toward humanity.** The animal path runs toward human form. A fox gains a tail per century; at some threshold of age and essence it can take human shape, speak, and join human society. This is not a trick of disguise but an actual *promotion in kind* — which is why fox-wives in the stories can truly love, and truly suffer, as humans do.

**4. Advancement is policed by heaven.** Great promotions provoke **tribulations (劫, *jie*)**: ordeals sent by the cosmic order to test the candidate — thunderbolts, temptations, ordeals of character. The snake-spirit who has cultivated a thousand years must survive heaven's lightning to take full human form. Fail, and centuries of work are burned away. The universe promotes on merit, but it *examines*.

**5. Karma outranks everything.** Beneath all mechanics lies the ledger: cause and effect across lives. Cultivation can change what you *are*; karma decides what *happens* to you. The tyrant king and the lovers' graves, the serpent fed on tribute-girls, the filial son visited by heaven — the stories insist, with monotonous moral seriousness, that no amount of power exempts anyone from the accounting. Even gods are audited.

That is the whole system: gather essence, pass the trials, and mind the ledger. Every marvel in this library is a special case of it.
