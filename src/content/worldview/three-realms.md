Every story in this library happens inside a **three-storey universe**. Not a hierarchy of reward and punishment — a *state*, with three floors, one set of laws, and constant unofficial traffic between levels.

That framing is the whole point. The Chinese cosmos is not a church. It is an administration with an upstairs, a mezzanine and a basement, and the interesting part is who gets a lift pass.

## 1. Heaven (天界): the upstairs office

Heaven is the palace of the Jade Emperor and his celestial bureaucracy: the star-gods in their constellations, the peach gardens of the [Queen Mother of the West](/worldview/queen-mother), the loom-house of the Weaver Maid. Heaven is imagined less as paradise than as a **court** — with ranks, posts, promotions, retirements and punishments, mirroring the imperial government below in exhausting detail.

Gods are, in effect, **officials**. This is why heaven can be petitioned, memoranda-ed, and occasionally defied. [The Thunder God Answers a Memo](/stories/the-thunder-god-answers-a-memo) treats a god's power as something a correctly-worded document can address.

## 2. The mortal realm (人间): the only floor where anything can change

The middle storey is the world of humans and everything living alongside them — animals, trees, rivers, mountains, and the spirits all of these may grow into. Its defining property is that **it is the only realm where change is possible**. A human may become an immortal; an animal may become a spirit; a ghost may be reborn.

That is why every story happens here. Heaven has finished developing; the underworld only processes. The mortal realm is the workshop, and everything the other two floors contain came up through it.

## 3. The underworld (地府): the basement office

Below is the [Courts of the Dead](/worldview/underworld): ten courts where every soul is judged, assigned its term, and eventually sent back upstairs to be reborn. It is not hell in the Christian sense — it is a processing centre, ruled by magistrates and clerks, as bureaucratic as heaven and considerably more overworked.

The two offices are not on speaking terms in the way you might expect. Heaven issues decrees; the underworld executes them; and both are answerable to the same **ledger**.

## 4. The traffic rules

The realms are not sealed, and crossing them is the engine of the whole genre. The Weaver Maid descends to marry a cowherd; a ghost climbs up to haunt a temple; a Taoist ascends in daylight; the Dragon Kings ride the rain between water-palaces and sky. [Climbing the Sunbeam](/stories/climbing-sunbeam) and [The Country Without Doors](/stories/the-country-without-doors) each describe one such border crossing and its consequences.

Most zhiguai plots can be summarised as **something crossed a border that is usually hard to cross** — and the drama comes from the rules that crossing breaks. Ask of any marvel: *which floor did it start on, and how did it get a permit?*

## 5. Two connective principles

Two ideas make the three floors one building rather than three.

- **Qi (气)**, the breath-stuff of which everything is made, flows through all three realms. The boundary is therefore a matter of **density rather than distance** — which is why a mountain can touch heaven and a corpse can rise from the earth.
- **Karma** is the passport system. What you did on one floor determines where you wake up on the next. Movement between realms is never free and never random; it is always, in the end, *accounted for*.

## 6. Hard rules for writing it

1. **Three floors, one law.** Never invent a separate metaphysics for heaven or the dead; they obey the same ledger.
2. **The middle floor is the stage.** If your plot needs change, it happens on earth, because only earth is unfinished.
3. **Every crossing needs a mechanism.** A road, a rope, a dream, a summons, a river — never a portal for its own sake.
4. **Heaven is a court, not a cloud.** Officials, ranks and memos, not harps.
5. **The underworld is a queue.** Souls wait, get filed, and eventually leave. It is a delay, not a destination.
