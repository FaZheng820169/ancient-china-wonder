In most modern fantasy, becoming more than human is a power curve. You train, you gain levels, you beat the next thing, you go up.

Chinese zhiguai describe something else entirely. The fullest statement of it in Qing fiction comes out of the mouth of a fox, in 《狐生員勸人修仙》 — and it is not mystical at all. It is administrative.

## There is a syllabus

The fox explains what a fox has to learn:

> 先學人形，再學人語。學人語者，先學鳥語；學鳥語者，又必須盡學四海九州之鳥語；無所不能，然後能為人聲，以成人形，其功已五百年矣。
>
> First learn the human shape, then learn human speech. To learn human speech, first learn bird speech — and to learn bird speech you must learn the speech of every bird within the four seas and the nine provinces. Only when there is nothing you cannot do can you produce a human voice and complete a human shape. That work alone is five hundred years.

And the total:

> 大率學仙者，千年而成，此定理也。
>
> Generally, those who study to be immortals take a thousand years to finish. This is a fixed principle.

Not "as long as it takes." A figure, stated as a principle.

## Your starting advantage is priced

The same speech then quantifies the head starts:

> 人學仙，較異類學仙少五百年功苦。若貴人、文人學仙，較凡人又省三百年功苦。
>
> A human studying immortality has five hundred years less labour than a non-human. And if an eminent man or a literary man studies it, he saves a further three hundred years over an ordinary person.

So the course is a thousand years, being born human is worth five hundred, and being born an eminent or educated human is worth another three hundred.

This is one of the most un-Western things in the whole tradition. The difficulty of the path is set by **what you were born as**, and the discount is published. Nobody in these stories argues that it is unfair.

## There is an annual exam, and failure is permanent

> 群狐蒙泰山娘娘考試，每歲一次。取其文理精通者為生員，劣者為野狐。生員可以修仙，野狐不許修仙。
>
> The foxes sit an examination held by the Lady of Mount Tai, once a year. Those whose composition is thoroughly competent become licentiates; the inferior ones become wild foxes. A licentiate may study to be an immortal. A wild fox may not.

Two things deserve attention. The examination is in **literary composition** — 文理, not power. And the consequence of failing is not repeating the year: you are reclassified as 野狐, wild fox, and **barred from studying immortality at all**.

The celestial bureaucracy runs a licensing exam, and the licence can be revoked permanently.

## The technique has a character requirement

《勞山道士》 is usually read as a joke about a lazy man. Read the master's instruction and it is a rule:

> The art only works for a heart that is quiet. Use it for mischief and it will fail you.

Wang cannot bear the months of axe-work and firewood, so he asks for one small trick instead — walking through walls. He is given the words, walks through the courtyard wall, and goes home pleased. His wife is unimpressed. He recites the spell, charges at the wall full of triumph, and:

> The wall, being a wall, stopped him.

He sits down hard with a lump on his forehead while his wife laughs until she cries, and concludes that the old Taoist cheated him.

He was not cheated. **The art was conditional on the state of his mind**, and he violated the condition in the same breath as using it. The failure is not that the magic stopped working; it is that the magic was never separable from whether he deserved it.

## The final interview cannot be passed by trying

《杜子春》 is the hardest story in this group and the most precise.

An old man gives Du Zichun money three times — thirty thousand, then a hundred thousand, then a million, then three million, each time saying *not enough* until he is satisfied. Later the same old man sets him to guard an alchemical furnace under one rule: **absolute silence**.

Du endures his own torture and his own death without a sound. He passes through the fear, the rage, the horror. Then his infant son is dashed against a stone, and he makes a noise.

The master's verdict:

> Greed, anger and fear are gone. Love remains.

That is the whole diagnosis, and it is not a consolation. The last attachment is not to self, or to pleasure, or even to love in the abstract — it is to a child, and it cannot be burned away by discipline. Du Zichun fails at the last gate, and the elixir is lost.

The story is Taoist in its machinery and Buddhist in its conclusion, and it says something rather severe: **the final requirement is to stop being human, and the thing that keeps you human is the last thing to go.**

## There is a shortcut, and it is admitted openly

Not everything takes a thousand years. 《帝流漿》 describes what the moon carries on the night of gengshen:

> 其形如無數橄欖，萬道金絲，累累貫串垂下。人間草木受其精氣即能成妖，狐狸鬼魅食之能顯神通。以草木有性無命，流漿有性，可以補命；狐狸鬼魅本自有命，故食之大有益也。
>
> Its form is like countless olives, with ten thousand threads of gold hanging strung together. Grass and trees that receive its essence can become sprites; foxes and ghosts that eat it can display divine powers. Because plants have nature but no life-span, the nectar has nature and can supply life-span; foxes and ghosts already have life, so eating it greatly benefits them.

This is a documented shortcut with a stated mechanism: external substance supplies the missing life-span. It is not cheating, and it is not hidden. It is simply one more entry in the same ledger.

## What success looks like

《趙三翁》 is the success case, and it is deliberately anticlimactic. Old Zhao is summoned to court at 108, cures the emperor's illness by laying mugwort on the patient's belly and letting summer sunlight through an opened roof-light, and when asked what he wants, asks for one thing: his discharge certificate from the river-works corvée.

The man who completed the path wants his paperwork.

## The shape of the thing

| Element | The rule |
| --- | --- |
| Duration | A thousand years, stated as a fixed principle |
| Prerequisite | Human shape first, then human speech — via every bird in the world |
| Starting discount | Human −500 years; eminent or educated −300 more |
| Licensing | Annual examination in composition, held by the Lady of Mount Tai |
| Failure | Reclassified as wild fox; barred from the path permanently |
| Conduct rule | Arts work only for a quiet heart; misuse voids them |
| Final gate | Silence under every provocation — including your own child |
| Shortcut | The nectar of gengshen, which supplies life-span |
| Reward | Whatever you ask for, if you have stopped wanting much |

What is striking is what is *not* in the table. There is no combat. There is no accumulation of power. There is no moment where the hero defeats a stronger enemy and levels up.

The path is closer to a civil service career than to an adventure — which is, of course, exactly what the stories say it is: an examination, a licence, a register, and a promotion ladder with a thousand-year probation period.

And the final gate is the cruellest design in the whole system. Everything else can be studied, bought down, or waited out. The last one is passable only by someone who has stopped being the kind of being that would want to pass it.
