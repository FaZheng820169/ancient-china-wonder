In the fantasy traditions that came west, weather is either indifferent physics or a god's mood. In the Chinese material it is neither. It is **the output of an office** — assigned to a post-holder, issued against a quota, sometimes dispatched in writing, and always, in principle, someone's fault.

That single shift changes what a storm is for in a story. Here is the evidence, in order.

## The rain has a portfolio holder

When a ghost appears to a magistrate in a Song collection, it explains itself in the language of a personnel file:

> 余非孫郎，乃漢大司馬董聖卿也。我為王莽所害，死甚慘。上帝憐我無罪……故封我為大郎神，管此方晴雨。

*I am not Lord Sun; I am Dong Shengqing, Grand Marshal of the Han. Wang Mang had me killed, and my death was a cruel one. God pitied me for being guiltless… and so enfeoffed me as Lord Dalang, and I manage the fine weather and the rain of this district.*

**管此方晴雨** — the sunshine and rain of this jurisdiction. Not the rain of the world; of *this jurisdiction*. The portfolio is bounded, like a magistrate's.

And a portfolio is staffed. In 《九龍廟》 a potter family works ten kilns, and nine of them turn out warped and rejected ware that nevertheless sells better than the good one. The reason, once a dragon explains it, is that nine dragon sons have been living under the nine kilns. For that favour they ask only premises — build us a shrine — and the reward is stated in the same administrative register: 其後以亢陽禱祈雨不擇日而降. Afterwards, in parching drought, when they prayed for rain it came **without a date being chosen**. Rain as a service level, delivered by a contractor with an office.

## The weather is dispatched in writing

The single best document in this whole literature is a river accident.

Three kinsmen of the imperial clan hire a boat and are crossing a stretch of river when a squall comes up at midday and something takes hold of the rudder. The boat goes over; the water is shallow enough that nobody drowns. On the bank, two crates drift in — first the uncle's patent of nobility, robe and boots, dry inside, then the nephews' commissions of appointment. Their other possessions do not come back. Later they find the goods in a ghost household, in daily use; and a Daoist master summons the spirits responsible.

The leader of the spirits turns out to be the old farmer they had met on the road that morning. He says:

> 某忝為當界土地，前此數日被城隍司公牒，指名覆此舟。諸物皆據牒交領，惟三人誥命及制書非籍中所載，旋送還之矣。牒見存，可以驗視。

*I am unworthily the earth god of this jurisdiction. Some days ago I received a public dispatch from the City God's office naming this boat to be capsized. Everything I took over, I took against the dispatch. Only the three men's patents of nobility and their letters of appointment were not entered in the schedule, and those I sent back at once. The dispatch is still here; you may examine it.*

He examines it. 竟無以罪之 — there was no way at all to charge him.

Read what that says about storms. The wreck was not weather; it was **an order**. It had an issuing office, a named target, an inventory schedule, and a clerk who could produce the file on request. The officer who executed it is inside his rights — indeed he was scrupulous, returning the two items that fell outside his schedule. The horror is not that a god is cruel. The horror is that the paperwork is *correct*.

The same logic runs through a milder case. In 《雷擊土地》 an earth god warns his old human friend, privately and at risk to himself, that the friend's mother is 分當雷擊 — it is her allotted portion to be struck by lightning. He cannot cancel it; his post is too small. There is exactly one remedy:

> 汝速盡孝養之道，凡太夫人平日一飲一饌、一帳一衣，務使十倍其數，浪費而暴餮之，庶几祿盡則亡，可以善終。

*Hurry and fulfil the way of filial support: everything the old lady ordinarily eats and drinks, every curtain and garment, see that it is ten times the amount — waste it, gorge on it — so that her ration runs out and she dies, and can end in peace.*

She dies a few years later. Three years after that the thunder finally comes: it circles the coffin, floods the room with the smell of sulphur, cannot get at the body, breaks out through the roof and destroys the earth god's shrine. **The bolt arrived on schedule. Only the address had been amended.**

## Lightning is served on a person

Note what the lightning is in that story: a **portion** (分), set against a ration (祿), delivered to a named individual on a date. Not a random strike with a moral attached. A delivery.

Which is why the thunder in these collections behaves like an officer with a route. It has a department, headed in the popular pantheon by the Thunder Ancestor; it strikes demons that have grown too large for their skins; and when it cannot reach the person it was sent for, it does not simply fail — it takes the nearest thing that will do, and leaves an earth-god's image in mud.

## Drought is not an absence. It is a body, and the body has an owner

The 《旱魃有三種》 account is explicit. There are three kinds of drought demon; one of them is 僵尸所變, a transformed corpse. The worst lives on mountains and is called 格:

> 似人而長頭，頂有一目，能吃龍，雨師皆畏之。見雲起，仰首吹噓，雲即散而日愈烈，人不能制。

*Man-shaped, with a long head and a single eye at the crown. It can eat dragons, and the rain-masters are all afraid of it. When it sees cloud rise it lifts its head and blows, and the cloud disperses and the sun grows fiercer; no one can control it.*

And the alternative doctrine in the same passage is even more administrative: 天應旱，則山川之氣融結而成 — when Heaven means there to be a drought, the vapour of the mountains and rivers congeals and becomes this thing. Either way, the dry season is not an absence of rain. It is a presence, with a location, and something has to be done about it.

Because it is a presence with an owner, prayer has a target — the new shrine, or Lord Dalang, or the dragon sons under the kilns. And because it is a presence, force is conceivable: the Zibuyu has a district that brings up cannon and fires at a locust swarm.

## Contempt for the office is punished with the office's instruments

The comedy of jurisdiction is 《湯顯祖》. The newly appointed magistrate refuses the customary bow at the local temple on the grounds that he outranks its occupant:

> 吾行五雷法，神祗在掌握中，豈當屈身拜於土偶之前。

*I practise the Five Thunder method. The gods are in the palm of my hand. How should I bend to a clay image?*

He has two tables set in the shrine and drinks with the god as host and guest, as an equal, and explains aloud that he expects the god's protection in return. That night a storm gets up, the hill water comes down, and the county office stands seven or eight feet under, up to the underside of his bed, documents and trunks half drowned. Nobody is hurt. 皆以為慢神之咎 — everyone took it for the fault of his contempt.

He is not struck down. He is **flooded, to exactly the level of his own desk.** The weather office answers, and answers in kind, and the answer is scaled to the insult.

## The correct method is not resistance, it is hospitality

The counter-example is 《蘇耽老飲疫神》. Walking out of his door at dawn, Su Danlao sees the plague god in the street, laughs, invites him in, seats him at the head of the table, and burns offerings for him.

That year the epidemic is bad, and the sick begin to speak in the god's voice:

> 我元旦受蘇耽老禮敬，愧無以報。欲禳我者，必請蘇君陪我，我方去。

*On New Year's Day I received Su Danlao's courtesy, and I am ashamed to have nothing to repay him. Anyone who wants to drive me off must ask Master Su to sit with me; then I will go.*

One dinner bought a seat at the bargaining table — and the god, bound by the debt, then negotiated on the whole district's behalf. This is the same transaction the magistrate in 《雷擊土地》 performs with a ration of food and cloth: find the quantity the weather office is working against, and pay it early.

## If you would rather kill the agent

《河朔漁叟》 is the other road. The Yellow River's embankment keeps bursting; a fisherman offers to dive, and comes up half a day later with a diagnosis:

> 下有長蛟為害，故埽不能堅，非殺之不可。

*There is a long jiao below doing the damage, so the fascines will not hold. It has to be killed.*

He asks for a sword from the prefect's store, goes down, and at noon the water turns red. He comes up with the dragon's head, and 洪流陡落 — the flood drops on the instant. Offered a military rank he refuses; offered gold he refuses; and within a short time he is dead. You may kill the agent of the storm. It costs what it costs.

## Six rules for writing weather in this universe

1. **Give the storm a portfolio holder.** Not a storm god — a post-holder with a bounded jurisdiction, a superior, and a reason to care about the outcome.
2. **Write the dispatch.** The best disaster here is an order with an inventory schedule. Let the players find the file.
3. **Let the paperwork be legitimate.** The earth god who sank the boat is not a villain and has nothing to be charged with. Procedural horror outlasts moral horror.
4. **Schedule it, then let them move the address.** A death-date with a name on it, plus a route to overspend the quota, is a whole scenario in three sentences.
5. **Drought wants a body.** Not a failed harvest — something on the mountain with one eye that eats dragons and can be found.
6. **Charge for killing the storm.** The fisherman refuses payment and dies days later. Weather's agent is not free to remove.

In the West, weather is what happens when nobody is in charge. Here it is what happens when somebody is — and he has a quota, a superior, a dispatch clerk, and a very bad temper.
